Sources

Every number on this site comes from one of the sources below. Each chart footer links here.

FRED economic data

Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (series originate at BLS, BEA, Board of Governors)

Measures
Fed funds rate, U-6 underemployment, information-sector employment, JOLTS aggregates
Caveat
Series are revised; we record the fetch date for every pull.
License
Public data; each series keeps its original publisher's terms
Retrieved
2026-10-10 (csv)
Link
https://fred.stlouisfed.org/

Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey (JOLTS)

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics

Measures
Openings, hires, layoffs and quits by industry, including information
Caveat
Openings count postings, not hiring intent. Latest month is preliminary. Industry detail has wider error margins.
License
Public domain (U.S. government work)
Retrieved
2026-10-10 (api)
Link
https://www.bls.gov/jlt/

Indeed Hiring Lab job postings tracker

Indeed Hiring Lab

Measures
Daily index of postings on Indeed by occupation, indexed to 1 Feb 2020 = 100
Caveat
An index relative to February 2020, not a count of jobs. Covers postings on Indeed only.
License
CC BY 4.0 (confirmed in the repo README; attribution required)
Retrieved
2026-10-10 (csv)
Link
https://github.com/hiring-lab/job_postings_tracker

Challenger, Gray & Christmas job cuts reports

Challenger, Gray & Christmas, Inc.

Measures
Announced job cuts by industry and stated reason, including cuts attributed to AI
Caveat
Announcements, not confirmed separations; reasons are as stated by the employer. AI has been tracked as its own reason only since May 2023, and 2024-25 reports often fold it into "Technological Updates", so AI counts are not comparable across years. Monthly tech-sector and AI figures are present only where the report text states them.
License
Copyrighted press releases; cite and link, do not redistribute
Retrieved
2026-10-10 (rss)
Link
https://www.challengergray.com/blog/category/job-cuts-report/

The Labor Market for Recent College Graduates

Federal Reserve Bank of New York

Measures
Unemployment and underemployment of recent graduates, and outcomes by college major including computer science
Caveat
Recent graduates are ages 22-27 with a bachelor's degree or higher. Outcomes by major are a single recent-years average, not a time series. Small samples for some majors.
License
Terms to be confirmed; cite and link
Retrieved
2026-10-10 (csv)
Link
https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/college-labor-market
